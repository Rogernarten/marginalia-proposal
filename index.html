Design, Build, Ship · Project Proposal · Yixiao Huang

Marginalia

For people who want to read a real book again but can't settle down: read a classic with every hard word and old custom explained in the margin, fitted to that sentence, so you never have to leave the page.

Who it's for

I talked to three readers on 3 November 2026: one who wants to read more but can't settle down, one literature student who reads old books all the time, and one who has read every night for half a year. We talked in Chinese; their words are translated, with the originals below.

“I scroll short videos until midnight, and the next day my brain feels gummed up. I can feel myself getting duller.”

“Now whenever I have a question I just ask AI. I can't even be bothered to think.”

“I've bought several books. Two pages in, I want to reach for my phone. I just can't settle down.”

每天刷短视频刷到半夜，第二天脑子像糊住了一样，我自己都能感觉到变钝了。现在有什么问题直接问 AI，连想都懒得想。买了好几本书，翻开两页就想摸手机，根本静不下来。

Kim, wants to read more

“Reading is part of my everyday life, but with old books the worst part is the notes. Half a page of notes for every page of text. By the time I come back from the notes, I've forgotten what the last sentence said.”

“Some words I look up in the dictionary, and when I put the meaning back in the text it still doesn't fit. The lookup was wasted.”

“Sometimes I skip the notes and push through, but afterwards I always feel I missed a lot.”

读书对我来说是日常，但读老书最头疼的是注释。一页正文半页注，看完注释回来，上一句说什么已经忘了。有些词查了字典，放回原文还是不对劲，等于白查。有时候干脆跳过注释硬读，读完又总觉得漏了很多。

Tom, literature student

“My rule is twenty minutes every night before bed. I've kept it up for more than half a year.”

“At first I forced myself with daily check-ins. Then it became a habit. Now I can't sleep if I don't read.”

“Never make the plan too big. I tried fifty pages a day and fell apart in three days.”

我给自己定的是每天睡前读二十分钟，坚持大半年了。一开始靠打卡逼自己，后来成习惯了，不读反而睡不着。计划千万别定太大。我试过一天五十页，三天就崩了。

Mike, reads every night

All three want to finish a good book, and stop for different reasons. Kim stops before starting: the phone is closer than the book. Tom stops in the middle: every trip to the notes or the dictionary breaks the sentence, and a dictionary meaning often doesn't fit the text. Mike shows what works: a small plan, kept every day.

Kim's worry about AI shapes the design: the app explains the word or line you chose, briefly, and stops. It never summarizes the book or reads it for you.

Focus: AI

A dictionary gives every meaning of a word; Tom needs the one that fits this sentence. That's what a model can do. When you select a word or a line, the app sends the model the sentence around it, and the model can call tools: Wikipedia (as Marginalia already does), a dictionary, and the text of the book up to where you are. It answers in the margin in a few lines, says which sources it used, and never mentions what happens later in the book.

Going deep means checking it. An eval set of hard passages from public-domain novels, each with the meaning that fits, runs on every change: is the meaning right for this sentence, is it short, does it cite a source, does it avoid spoilers.

Roadmap

Nov 10–17Riskiest part first: the eval set (30 passages from Pride and Prejudice) and the explain call with its tools, until it passes. Then put it in the margin of Marginalia, where sign-up, the book and the notebook already work.
Nov 17 · v1A new reader signs up, opens Pride and Prejudice, selects a word or line they don't understand, reads an explanation that fits that sentence without leaving the page, and saves it to their notebook.
Nov 17–24Mike's small daily goal: choose 10 or 20 minutes, check in each day, see the streak. More books, and more passages in the evals.
Nov 24No class (Thanksgiving). Kim, Tom and Mike use it for a week; I watch where they stop.
Dec 1 · v2A quiet reading mode for Kim: one short section at a time, with the margin and the timer and nothing else. Fixes from the week of testing.
Left outBook summaries or a chat that answers anything about the book (the app explains, it doesn't read for you). A phone app. Book clubs and sharing. Books still under copyright.

How it's built

Next.js on VercelWhat Marginalia already runs on. The model is called from a server route, so its key never reaches the browser.
ClerkSign-up and sign-in, already set up for Marginalia.
SupabasePostgres with row-level security for the notebook, reading position and check-ins: each reader sees only their own. Explanations are saved per passage, so the same question never calls the model twice.
Model APIChosen in week 1 by the evals: the cheapest model that passes, on a free tier or with a spending cap.
Model toolsWikipedia's REST API, Wiktionary, and the book's own text from Project Gutenberg.
MCP serversSupabase MCP, for migrations and reading logs.
CLIsVercel CLI for deploys, environment variables and logs; Clerk CLI for the auth setup.
